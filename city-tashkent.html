A capital of two faces — Soviet-era metro mosaics and bustling bazaars beside gleaming towers of glass and steel.

Tashkent is the largest city in Central Asia and the principal gateway to Uzbekistan. Rebuilt after the 1966 earthquake, it blends wide leafy boulevards and grand Soviet-modernist architecture with one of the world's most beautiful metro systems — each station a gallery of marble, mosaic and chandeliers.

Today the skyline rises again at Tashkent City, a district of parks, fountains and towers, while the Chorsu Bazaar and old town keep the capital rooted in tradition. Most journeys through Uzbekistan begin and end here.

Where the Silk Road meets the 21st century.

FAQ

How many days do I need in Tashkent?+
One to two days is enough for the metro, bazaars and Tashkent City — most travellers use it as the arrival and departure hub.

Absolutely — photography is now permitted and the ornate stations are an attraction in their own right.
