The Registan

Three towering madrasahs of tile and gold — the ceremonial heart of Timurid Samarkand and the emblem of Uzbekistan.

The story Gallery Visiting Nearby FAQ

"Registan" means "sandy place" — but for six centuries this has been the most magnificent public square in Central Asia. Framed by three madrasahs built between the 15th and 17th centuries, it served as a bazaar, a place of royal proclamation, and a centre of learning.

The Ulugh Beg Madrasah (1420) was raised by Timur's grandson, the astronomer-king, and once taught mathematics and astronomy. Facing it, the Sher-Dor Madrasah (1636) is famous for its tigers-and-sun mosaic — a rare depiction of living creatures in Islamic art. Between them, the Tilya-Kori Madrasah (1660) dazzles with a gold-leafed prayer hall.

An ensemble that codified the visual language of the Islamic world.

Restored across the 20th and 21st centuries, the Registan today is floodlit at night and hosts seasonal sound-and-light shows.

Planning your visit

Best at golden hour — arrive early morning or before sunset for the warmest light and fewest crowds.
Allow 1.5–2 hours to explore all three madrasahs and climb a minaret (seasonal).
Dress modestly — shoulders and knees covered; it remains an active heritage site.

FAQ

Is there an entrance fee?+
Yes, a single ticket covers all three madrasahs. Night sound-and-light shows are ticketed separately in season.
Can I go inside the madrasahs?
Yes — the courtyards house artisan workshops and the gilded Tilya-Kori prayer hall is open to visitors.
