The crossroads of cultures — where Timur's empire rose in turquoise, gold and the world's most dazzling tilework.

Samarkand is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Central Asia, a jewel of the Silk Road that flourished as the capital of Amir Timur's empire in the 14th and 15th centuries. Its monuments — clad in cobalt and turquoise faience — defined an architectural language copied from Delhi to Istanbul.

Today the city pairs its UNESCO-listed core with a modern face: a striking new airport, high-speed rail to Tashkent and Bukhara, and a polished tourism complex. It is the natural centrepiece of any Uzbekistan itinerary.

Top sights

Must-see

Registan Square

Three monumental madrasahs framing the grandest public square of the Islamic world.

Shah-i-Zinda

An avenue of mausoleums lined with breathtaking glazed tilework.

Gur-e-Amir

The fluted blue dome over the tomb of Amir Timur himself.

Bibi-Khanym Mosque

Once among the largest mosques in the world, beside the Siab bazaar.

Spring (Apr–May) — mild days, blossoming orchards and ideal light for photography.
