Itchan Kala

Khiva's walled inner city — a complete medieval town of mud-brick walls, turquoise minarets and silent caravanserais.

The story Gallery Visiting Nearby FAQ

Itchan Kala is the walled inner town of the old Khiva oasis — the last stop for caravans before the desert crossing to Iran. Enclosed by 10-metre brick ramparts, it is the first site in Uzbekistan inscribed by UNESCO, in 1990, and the best-preserved example of a Central Asian Islamic city.

Inside the walls stand more than fifty monuments: the unfinished, fat turquoise stump of the Kalta Minor minaret; the Kunya Ark fortress; the Tash Hauli Palace with its painted courtyards; and the slender Islam Khoja minaret, the tallest in Khiva. Few cars, few wires — at dusk it feels untouched by time.

A whole medieval city, preserved within its walls.

Compact and entirely walkable, Itchan Kala is best explored slowly across a full day, ideally with a night inside the walls.

FAQ

Do I need a ticket for Itchan Kala?+
Entry to the walled city is free, but a combined ticket gives access to the museums, minarets and palaces inside.
How long should I spend in Khiva?
A full day covers the highlights; staying a night inside the walls lets you enjoy the city at dawn and dusk without crowds.
